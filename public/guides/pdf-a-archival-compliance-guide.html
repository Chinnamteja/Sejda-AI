PDF/A Archival Compliance Guide: Standards (1b, 2b, 3b) for 50-Year Document Preservation

Digital files are notoriously fragile. Software evolves, proprietary fonts become extinct, operating systems deprecate legacy graphics drivers, and obsolete compression algorithms cease to be supported. A standard PDF created today might fail to render accurately—or even open at all—thirty or fifty years from now.

To solve this existential challenge for government registries, national libraries, patent offices, and judicial archives, the International Organization for Standardization developed the PDF/A standard (ISO 19005). In this guide, we explain the architectural constraints of PDF/A and how to ensure your legal and historical records remain readable for generations to come.

The Cardinal Rule of PDF/A: 100% Self-Containment

The central premise of PDF/A is that a document must contain every single component necessary to reproduce its visual appearance identically on any future computing system, without relying on external fonts, operating system libraries, or internet connectivity.

To guarantee self-containment, the PDF/A specification strictly enforces:

  • Mandatory Font Embedding: 100% of all glyphs and font programs must be embedded inside the file. System fonts cannot be referenced externally.
  • Device-Independent Color Profiles: Colors must be defined using standard ICC color profiles (e.g. sRGB or ISO Coated) to avoid color drift on future displays.
  • Prohibition of Dynamic Content: JavaScript, audio, video, and executable attachments are strictly forbidden because their runtime engines cannot be guaranteed decades into the future.
  • Prohibition of Encryption: Password-protected or encrypted files are banned under PDF/A to ensure future archivists are never locked out of historical public records.

Strict Technical Constraints of PDF/A Archival

Converting a document to valid PDF/A format requires satisfying rigorous validation tests:

  • Mandatory Embedded Fonts: Every typeface used must be 100% embedded with complete unicode mapping tables (ToUnicode).
  • Device-Independent Color Space: All RGB and CMYK colors must be bound to standardized ICC color profiles defined in the document's /OutputIntents dictionary.
  • No Encryption or Passwords: Archival repositories must never be locked behind proprietary encryption algorithms that could be lost to posterity.

The Engineering Mechanics of ISO 19005 PDF/A Compliance

The PDF/A standard was created jointly by the Association for Information and Image Management (AIIM), the NPES, and the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts to resolve a fundamental flaw in standard PDF: lack of guaranteed self-containment. In standard PDFs, applications can rely on "system fonts" or external rendering engines. If an archival repository opens that file on a server thirty years later, the absence of those specific proprietary fonts results in font substitution, scrambled line wraps, and unreadable court decrees.

PDF/A transforms a document into a completely closed computational ecosystem. Every typeface must embed its full character metrics, glyph outlines, and ToUnicode mapping CMap dictionaries. Furthermore, color reproduction is strictly device-independent: all color coordinates must be tethered to an embedded International Color Consortium (ICC) profile registered in the /OutputIntents dictionary.

Troubleshooting Common PDF/A Conversion Rejections

Documents frequently fail PDF/A validation due to two obscure technical issues:

  • Missing CIDSet and CharSet Dictionaries: Older PDF generators often omit the /CIDSet stream in embedded TrueType/OpenType fonts, causing strict validators to reject the file. Sejda automatically synthesizes compliant CIDSet streams during conversion.
  • Device-Dependent Black Points in Grayscale Images: When converting grayscale photos, uncalibrated generators often omit gray ICC profiles (such as Gray Gamma 2.2). Sejda automatically injects standardized ICC color spaces into all device streams.
