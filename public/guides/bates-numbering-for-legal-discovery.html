Bates Numbering for Legal Discovery: Formatting, Prefixing & Court Bundle Production

In formal litigation, arbitration, regulatory investigations, and courtroom trials, legal teams must exchange thousands of evidentiary documents. To ensure attorneys, judges, witnesses, and court reporters can refer to any specific piece of evidence without ambiguity, every single page must be sequentially stamped with a standardized, tamper-evident reference identifier known as a Bates Number.

In this guide, we review the conventions governing legal Bates stamping, formatting standards, and how to apply sequential Bates numbers across multiple PDF files using Sejda.

Anatomy of a Professional Bates Stamp

A compliant Bates stamp typically contains three distinct components:

  1. Alphanumeric Prefix: Identifies the producing party or case identifier (e.g. PLAINTIFF_, DEF_SMITH_, or SEC-INV_).
  2. Zero-Padded Sequential Number: Standard practice mandates 6 to 8 digit padding (e.g. 000001 through 014890) to prevent sorting errors in document databases.
  3. Confidentiality Suffix (Optional): Stamped alongside the number when subject to protective orders (e.g. - CONFIDENTIAL - ATTORNEYS EYES ONLY).

Legal Discovery Numbering Standards & Federal Rules Compliance

In state and federal litigation, parties are required to exchange document productions with indelible, sequential identifiers known as Bates numbering. Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure (FRCP) Rule 34 and local district court rules, unpaginated or inconsistently stamped discovery bundles can lead to judicial sanctions, motion to compel orders, or exhibit disqualification at trial.

Best Practices for Multi-Volume Production

When preparing electronic discovery bundles across hundreds of witness files, adhere to these procedural rules:

  • Maintain Unbroken Continuity: If Exhibit 4 ends on page ABC_000342, Exhibit 5 must begin precisely at ABC_000343 regardless of source document boundaries.

Litigation Discovery: Sequential Bates Stamping & Exhibit Production

The Legal History and Mandatory Role of Bates Numbering in Litigation

Named after Edwin G. Bates who patented the mechanical numbering machine in 1891, Bates numbering is the universally mandated method of indexing electronic discovery (ESI) in civil litigation, criminal proceedings, and government regulatory audits (including US SEC, FTC, and DOJ investigations).

In complex litigation involving hundreds of thousands of internal corporate emails, technical schematics, and financial spreadsheets, attorneys cannot simply cite "Page 4" in open court because different print drivers render page breaks differently. By stamping every single sheet of paper and electronic page with a unique, alphanumeric identifier (e.g. DEFENDANT-004821), counsel, judicial clerks, and the trial judge can reference the exact piece of evidence instantaneously during cross-examination without confusion.
